## Abstract

World Action Models jointly generate future video and robot actions through iterative denoising. Few-step distillation reduces the number of model evaluations, but its coarse updates can move the student away from the teacher’s trajectory. We propose **Anchored Path Matching (APM)**, which supervises endpoint and short-transition predictions from states reached by the student. From each state, we continue the teacher to zero noise and use its endpoint and first intermediate state as targets. Modality-routed adapters learn to match both targets from the same starting state. When the two gradients conflict, anchor-priority updates preserve the endpoint gradient and remove the opposing component of the local gradient within each modality. One checkpoint supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step joint generation. On RoboTwin 2.0, APM improves single-step success over continued distillation by 2.0 percentage points on Clean and 1.4 points on Random. On two real-robot tasks, it improves success over the one-step teacher and reduces control-cycle latency by 21.3× relative to the eight-step teacher.

<h2 id="method">One teacher continuation. Two complementary targets.</h2>

APM first learns a finite-interval video–action transition map through teacher-integrated distillation. It then freezes the base and trains modality-routed adapters to correct predictions from states encountered along the student’s own trajectories.

<h2 id="video">Better video predictions at one step.</h2>

Compared with continued Stage I distillation, APM increases single-step PSNR from **30.169 to 32.317 dB**, alongside improvements in SSIM and LPIPS. The evaluation uses 500 shared contexts with ground-truth observation histories. Against Flash-WAM under each model’s deployment configuration, APM has higher PSNR; Flash-WAM has better SSIM and LPIPS.
